<h2 id=""><strong id="">Overview - Piers Ridyard, CEO</strong></h2><p id="">On February 18th we kicked off a competition for the community to build example implementations of decentralized exchanges (like Uniswap) using Scrypto. This was the first little test of the power of Scrypto and what is being delivered by the incredible dev team here at RDX Works, and the results were pretty awesome.</p><p id="">Although still early in the life of the fledgling language, Scrypto continues to provide glimpses into what an incredible competitive advantage to a DeFi project on Radix it will be&nbsp; - not only reducing the time to go from idea to fully working code, but also increasing the complexity and intelligence that can be applied to the design of a DeFi system in the first place.</p><p id="">Here are a couple of quotes from the winning participants about their experience building, but I strongly encourage you to go and give it a try for yourself, it’s a pretty rewarding experience, even if you are doing it for fun: <a id="" href="https://developers.radixdlt.com/">https://developers.radixdlt.com/</a></p><p id=""><em id="">[It took me] maybe about 8 working hours coding after 2 hours of planning to get the majority of the DEX developed (not including comments/docs/tests).&nbsp; That's kind of mindbogglingly efficient to get the idea on the table.&nbsp; I absolutely spent more time trying to figure out the inconsequential crypto library I used (which was poorly documented) than doing any Scrypto-specific on-ledger development. </em>- devmannic</p><p id=""><em id="">For approximately 2 months now, I’ve been learning Scrypto and using it to build various dApps and I’m blown away by Scrypto and the Radix Engine as a whole! I believe that the fact that an AMM DEX can be implemented in 2 weeks or so speaks for how powerful yet simple and welcoming Scrypto is.</em> - 0xOmar</p><p id=""><em id="">While I was coding ChainDEX I found Scrypto to be powerful and easy to learn. I come from a background of Java and C so it took some getting used to the pickiness of the language, but once I did I found the compiler to be very helpful. It feels hard to do things wrong. I think the asset-oriented paradigm is easy to understand and works well, but some features do still need to be added to make sure there are no limitations. Which is understandable considering Scrypto is only at v0.3!</em> - FR05T8YTE</p><p id="">To learn more and see the actual code produced, please jump in here - <a id="" href="https://www.radixdlt.com/post/scrypto-dex-challenge-results">https://www.radixdlt.com/post/scrypto-dex-challenge-results</a></p><p id="">Huge shoutout to devmanic, 0xOmar, FR05T8YTE and everyone else in the Scrypto community that participated in the competition directly, or who has been hanging out on our community Discord and writing the docs and answering the questions and generally providing the incredible, welcoming support that makes all of this possible in the first place.</p><p id="">You are the real heroes here.</p><h2 id=""><strong id="">Strategy &amp; Marketing (Adam Simmons, CSO &amp; Jeremy Epstein, CMO)</strong>&nbsp;</h2><figure id="" class="w-richtext-figure-type-image w-richtext-align-center" data-rt-type="image" data-rt-align="center"><div id=""><img id="" src="/assets/624f092cc6c5a96add1e0698_02TYWR8ls5gWLk9njvQJvLSQ5rAbCetFLMaGTemO6Mp9Kg0-J3QSmMMoIIR-ehWWnOupYhHAYotxj_UW4t6czonrYS1NnMp5gnIZFD5z9bMPNPz5S4soIAb_9dsjJxTjaI0aXtfc.jpeg" width="auto" height="auto" loading="auto"></div></figure><p id="">I wish everyone could have been at our DC RadixRevelation Meet Up last week. Babylon represents a host of interdependent product releases – core node, gateway, Radix Engine, Scrypto, and Babylon wallet (name TBD) – and so development efforts are starting to transition toward building the first pieces of the skeleton of the combined system and defining the interfaces between products and teams for Babylon.</p><p id="">Work is beginning on restructuring the core node for Babylon-style state, with transitions defined by the new transaction manifest and running through the new Radix Engine v2 asset rules. This is the beginning of a road that will culminate in Alphanet in the back half of the year, when we’ll have the first real functional network with REv2.</p><p id="">Meanwhile, parallel work continues on Olympia network enhancements, including coordinated forking which is nearing completion.</p><p id="">Scrypto work continues apace, as we drive toward our next big release with a much updated authorization model that we’re very excited about, plus other enhancements. We’ve begun applying this new auth philosophy to control of resources as well, which is feeling very nice.</p><p id="">Babylon wallet work continues in strict stealth mode, with the primary focus at this stage on technical research on some new syncing and cryptography tools that we’ll need in our toolbox, as well as ongoing UX research which is showing strong promise; it feels like we’re really onto something with what the future of the web3 user experience should be.</p><p id="">Meanwhile, another Olympia wallet update is in the works.
