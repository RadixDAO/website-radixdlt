Alongside this, we released a video for “The Problems with Smart Contracts Today” which already has over 1300 views on Youtube.</li><li id="">To aid understanding within the Radix community towards some of the more complex things the Radix team deal with, we launched the <a id="" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bLPLY70ywt0">Round Table discussion</a> with Piers, Russell, Matt, and Adam. For some background on the tech behind Cassandra Dan and CEO of Radix Piers <a id="" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1rNeL-X40lc">recorded an hour long discussion</a> on the what and how of the Web3 Twitter demo.&nbsp;</li></ul><p id=""><br></p><h2 id="">Development (<a id="" href="mailto:russell@radixdlt.com">Russell Harvey</a>, CTO &amp; <a id="" href="mailto:matt@radixdlt.com">Matthew Hine</a>, Head of Product - Radix DLT)</h2><p id="">Usually in this space we talk about our ongoing work on the Olympia mainnet and associated apps like the Radix Desktop Wallet. But at the same time, furious work proceeds on our next big release milestone, Alexandria. While Alexandria won’t bring a change to mainnet, it will give developers first access to early forms of Scrypto and Radix Engine v2, running in a simulator environment on their machine.</p><p id="">Over the past several months, the team has taken Scrypto from concept to a working, usable language that compiles, runs, and is letting us quickly iterate on. We’ve remained believers in the value of an “asset-oriented” alternative to smart contract development, but finally putting our hands on actually building things this way feels great.</p><p id="">The Scrypto compiler adapts Rust’s compiler by adding the variety of Radix Engine-specific features for assets and other new functions that Scrypto exposes through a range of new syntax. The initial form of the simulator environment contained a very basic form of that adapted compiler, and the experience was raw, but it allowed us to compile simple Scrypto code through a form of Radix Engine. From that basis, we have been progressively adding features and&nbsp; building out a command line tool that allows the developer to simulate things that would be part of an actual network, like set up accounts and tokens, instantiate blueprints into components, push transactions through them, and read the state of components.</p><p id="">We’ve still got our work cut out for us for the next month until our Alexandria Preview Event where we’ll show our progress to this point for the first time! But everything is looking good for an Alexandria release this year that will show our community a new way of building smart contracts that we’re very excited about.</p><p id=""><br></p>
