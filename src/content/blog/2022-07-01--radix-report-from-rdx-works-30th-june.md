<h4 id=""><strong id="">Overview - Piers Ridyard, CEO</strong></h4><p id="">‍</p><p id="">While the sunshine of Malibu and sweat of Austin are now a thing of (near) past, the effects of the FooHack and Consensus are not. If I were ever in doubt that Scrypto is one of the most important and needful things in the crypto industry today, I am no longer.</p><p id="">When we set out to create a programming language that made getting new developers into Web3.0 and DeFi as easy as possible, we knew it was a daunting task. Getting a multi-platinum selling musician from zero to Scrypto competent in less than three months, and then helping to create a decentralized chess game in less than 1 week has exceeded every hope we had for it.</p><p id="">Note - this is more complicated than almost every DeFi dApp currently on Ethereum. To put it in perspective, an entire class of German computer science students tried to do this using <a id="" href="https://medium.com/@graycoding/lessons-learned-from-making-a-chess-game-for-ethereum-6917c01178b6">Solidity and failed</a>.</p><p id="">A man without any formal coding experience, who made global hits like Party Rock Anthem, out-coded a class at an elite technical university. While Redfoo is awesome, that is some seriously incredible testimony for Scrypto.</p><figure id="" class="w-richtext-figure-type-video w-richtext-align-center" style="padding-bottom:33.723653395784545%" data-rt-type="video" data-rt-align="center" data-rt-max-width="" data-rt-max-height="33.723653395784545%" data-rt-dimensions="854:480" data-page-url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KQ6zr6kCPj8"><div id=""><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/KQ6zr6kCPj8" scrolling="no" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen="true"></iframe></div></figure><p id="">However, Redfoo is not the only story like that, Peter Kim’s story is equally awesome, and those who missed it can see our writeup about his journey into Scrypto<a id="" href="https://www.radixdlt.com/post/community-spotlight-peter-kim-the-scrypto-oracles-challenge"> here</a>.</p><p id="">And, of course, Consensus. The thing I heard repeatedly was the things that we already suspected were true: Solidity and the EVM have a large number of problems, other networks are little better to build on, and there is a huge need for more tech talent in the industry.</p><p id="">We are still digging, but it is now clear that here be solid gold.</p><p id="">‍</p><h4 id=""><strong id="">Strategy &amp; Marketing (Adam Simmons, CSO &amp; Jeremy Epstein, CMO)</strong>&nbsp;</h4><p id="">‍</p><p id="">The success of the FooHack, as you read <a id="" href="https://www.radixdlt.com/post/scrypto-is-as-smooth-as-vegan-butter">about yesterday</a>, brings with it a lot of potential and a bit of pressure. The potential comes from having a world-famous rockstar as a committed Radix evangelist on the team.&nbsp; The pressure now is on the Marketing team to make sure we leverage this asset in a way that helps drive awareness and perception of the Radix brand.&nbsp; <br><br>The good news is that we are in a position to do that, thanks to the tremendous help of so many people throughout RDX Works and our talented community members who were able to participate. Over the weeks to come, with both our own efforts, as well as through influencer and PR channels, we will seek to capitalize on this unique angle.</p><p id="">For starters, take a look at the <a id="" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dtRSXtblWUA">FooHack trailer video</a>. <br><br>Lastly, we spent the last 2 weeks solidifying our individual and team OKRs for Q3.&nbsp; I’m so proud of every member of the team who has committed to the OKR process—which isn’t easy to do–and who focused on driving their outcomes.&nbsp; I’ll share more in the next Marketing Wen-sday update next week.</p><p id="">‍</p><h4 id=""><strong id="">Development (Russell Harvey, CTO &amp; Matthew Hine, CPO)</strong></h4><p id="">‍</p><p id="">The forges are lit, the pipes are skirling away, a great drum beats in the distance, and Babylon implementation marches along, with our battle-hardened team of developers laboring away in their tireless pursuit of a functional internal test network.&nbsp; Watching the tech team channels is like sitting in Mission Control during the Apollo missions.&nbsp; A problem appears, there’s a flurry of activity as everyone coordinates a resolution, and then it’s back to the comforting hum of smooth operations.</p><p id="">One item in particular that I want to discuss is our progress on fees for Babylon.&nbsp; You can think of support for fees as being divided into two main efforts.&nbsp; The first is around <em id="">instrumentation and metering</em>, that is, having the capability of calculating the cost of running a particular segment of code (which, in the case of Radix, is also where royalties are accounted for).&nbsp; The second is around the <em id="">payment</em> of those costs—making the system able to understand how those costs should be paid, claiming the appropriate fee, and ensuring that execution does not exceed the fee available to be paid.&nbsp; Implementation of the instrumentation part of fees is quite far along at this point, and once we tie off the rest of the initial work there we’ll move on to the payment side of things.&nbsp; TL;DR, we are targeting putting a larval implementation of fees into developers’ hands with Scrypto v0.5, though no promises at this point.</p><p id="">Don’t forget that the <a id="" href="https://www.radixdlt.com/post/scrypto-lending-challenge-is-live">Scrypto Lending Challenge</a> is live!&nbsp;
