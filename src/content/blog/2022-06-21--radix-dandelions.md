Most of the time it doesn’t pay off.&nbsp;</p><p id="">In short, it’s simply not a feasible or judicious marketing tactic to deploy.</p><p id="">The second most common answer to the “massive awareness” problem is to “do something that goes viral”. The problem is that there’s no way to actually make something “go viral.” <em id="">&nbsp;</em></p><p id=""><em id="">[PSA: If a marketer, consultant, or agency ever says “oh, we make viral videos,” walk the other way.]</em></p><p id="">‍</p><h4 id="">Ways to Brand Virality</h4><p id="">‍</p><p id="">While it’s not possible to engineer virality, it <strong id="">is</strong> possible to <em id="">increase the odds of going viral.</em>&nbsp;</p><p id="">The way to do that is to try a LOT of things quickly and cheaply.</p><p id="">Our inspiration comes from the humble dandelion.&nbsp;</p><p id="">About 14 years ago, I wrote a book called <strong id="">“Dandelion Marketing</strong>” (<a id="" href="https://www.dropbox.com/s/a5tlwwqor9ztdgn/Dandelion%20Marketing.pdf?dl=0">PDF</a>). The premise of the book was that the best way to do marketing that might go viral was to emulate nature’s approach to successful proliferation.</p><p id="">The dandelion flower creates hundreds of little seeds, at exceedingly low cost, and then casts them to the wind.&nbsp; Most of them don’t make it, but enough do to make the “marketing” efforts successful.</p><p id="">And if you think about it, this is how human reproduction works. All it takes is one, right?&nbsp;</p><p id="">So, that’s what we are going to do in the next step of our community marketing empowerment efforts.</p><p id="">‍</p><h4 id="">Ways to Radix Awareness</h4><p id="">‍</p><p id="">We’ve seen that members of the community have a TON of incredibly creative and exciting marketing ideas that may be worthy of experimentation.
